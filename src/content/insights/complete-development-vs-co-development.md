<h2>The short answer</h2>
<p>Choose <strong>complete development</strong> when you need someone else to own delivery. Choose <strong>co-development</strong> when you already own delivery and need more hands or specific skills inside your team.</p>
<h2>Complete development</h2>
<p>With complete development, the partner plans the project, builds and manages the team, runs production and delivers the finished game. You stay responsible for the vision, approvals and business decisions.</p>
<p>It usually fits when:</p>
<ul><li>you are a founder or publisher without an internal engineering team</li><li>you have a clear concept or design document and need it turned into a product</li><li>you want one accountable party for schedule and quality</li></ul>
<p>The trade-off is control over day-to-day decisions. You review milestones and builds rather than individual tasks, so good documentation and fast feedback matter.</p>
<h2>Co-development</h2>
<p>With co-development, specialists or a small team join your production. They work in your repository, follow your code standards and take tasks from your backlog. Your leads stay in charge.</p>
<p>It usually fits when:</p>
<ul><li>you have a studio and a pipeline but not enough capacity for a milestone</li><li>you are missing a specific skill, such as multiplayer or optimisation</li><li>you want to scale up for a phase without permanent hiring</li></ul>
<p>The trade-off is management time. Your team still plans the work and reviews it, so co-development works best when you have someone able to lead the extra people.</p>
<h2>Questions that decide it</h2>
<ol><li><strong>Who will write the tasks?</strong> If nobody on your side can, you need complete development.</li><li><strong>Where does the code live?</strong> If it must stay in your repository and follow your standards, co-development is simpler.</li><li><strong>Who is accountable for the release date?</strong> If you want that to be the partner, choose complete development.</li><li><strong>How long do you need help?</strong> A single milestone often suits co-development; a whole product usually suits complete development.</li></ol>
<h2>A hybrid is common</h2>
<p>Many projects move between the two. A partner may build the first playable version under complete development and then continue as a co-development team once you hire internal leads. Agree how that handover would work at the start.</p>
